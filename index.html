THE PROBLEM

You treat LinkedIn like a content calendar, not a trust engine.

For Companies

Deals are shaped before the sales meeting.

Every company has a LinkedIn page. Most post regularly. But posting and being known are two very different things.

Talent forms an opinion before reading the job description. Investors judge the company and its leaders before the first conversation.

The companies that win are not always the ones posting the most. They are the ones people already know, trust, and have an opinion about before the meeting begins.

For Executives

The expertise is real. The profile does not show it.

Decades of insight are trapped in meetings, notes, panels and private conversations.

Posting depends on limited time, so the voice appears inconsistently or not at all.

The content sounds generic, overly corporate, or unlike the person behind it.

Case File — 001

Arun Maira

FORMER CHAIRMAN, BCG INDIA

Arun Maira — photographed for RocketRush

Goal

Get more people to read his books, and spread the ideas to the people who could act on them — policymakers, students, institutions.

What We Did

  • Drew stories from his work with Tata, BCG, and the Government of India — decisions made, transformations witnessed, lessons that never made it into the official record.
  • Built content that worked at two levels simultaneously: deep enough for policymakers, accessible enough for students encountering these ideas for the first time.
  • Turned existing articles and panel discussions into formats that made people feel the idea before they analysed it.
  • Created a consistent presence that put his books in context — not as products to sell, but as the natural next step for anyone who'd been following his thinking.
